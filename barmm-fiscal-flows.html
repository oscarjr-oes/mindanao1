The Special Fund loop

The one circularity lever that is measured, not modelled

Reversion and re-appropriation, cohort by cohort

Under RA 11054 Article XII §19, any unspent block grant reverts to the Bangsamoro Treasury under a Special Fund for re-appropriation. Reversion is automatic; re-appropriation is not. That makes the Special Fund a queue — and a queue can be measured.

Reversion rate by fiscal year

Full bar is the unobligated balance; solid is what reverted to the Special Fund; hatched is unobligated but not reverted.

The reversion line is absent from the source table for FY2023–25 — Bangsamoro Autonomy Acts 38 and 52 extended appropriation validity. "Provisional" means the column is not fully as of 31 Dec 2025.
The loop does not close, and this panel does not claim it does. ₱30,299,345,504.38 reverted; ₱30,289,943,558.24 came back out. The ₱9.4M difference is not rounding, not a transcription error and not a scope difference — the source's own vintage sub-rows split it into two cohort residuals (FY2020 ₱7,100,000.00 exactly; FY2021 ₱2,301,946.14) while FY2022 closes to the centavo. What the source does not say is why. So the claim made here is the defensible one: 99.969% of reverted funds are traced to a named re-appropriation year, and ₱9.4M is not.
The bigger finding: the reversion mechanism has been legislated away. Reversion covered 100% of the unobligated balance in FY2020 and FY2021, 45.9% in FY2022, and 0% in FY2023–25 — for those years the reversion line is absent from the table structure entirely, not reported as zero. Bangsamoro Autonomy Act No. 38 keeps FY2020–22 appropriations effective until fully utilised, and No. 52 extends the FY2023 GAA's validity. Extending appropriation validity suspends the §19 lapse that triggers reversion. Whether that helps or hurts circularity is a genuine open question: extended validity keeps money with the implementing ministry and skips the two-to-three-year queue, but removes Parliament's re-allocation checkpoint. Both readings fit the data.

Velocity is measured to annual resolution only, and the panel stops there. The matrix gives a real measured lag — 2.67 fiscal years weighted mean, with 65.9% of re-appropriated money returning at exactly a three-year lag and none faster than two. That floor is structural: a year must close before its underspend is known, and re-appropriating it needs a parliamentary appropriation. Two things are not measurable and have not been modelled to fill the gap. Re-appropriation is not re-obligation — once re-appropriated, money is pooled into the year's fund sources and no published figure separates it from that year's own SARO releases. Sub-annual timing needs Special Fund SARO dates; sixteen were found for FY2024 (see the panel above), which narrows the gap without closing it. With three completed cohorts and five observed cells, fitting a lag distribution would have more parameters than data.

Circularity & leakage

Does a peso spent here stay here?
How to read this section. Solid fills are measured figures. Hatched fills are modelled — a scenario, not a measurement. Everything downstream of the principal in the leakage model below is hatched for that reason.

The leakage model

Government spending is around 36% of the BARMM economy. With 91 unbanked municipalities and thin local intermediation, a peso spent may leave the region within one transaction cycle rather than turning over locally. This traces one year's capital outlay through three rounds — the contract, then wages and subcontracts, then household spend — splitting retained from leaked at each.

Where the defaults come from, and what is deliberately absent ▾

Why capital outlay as principal. barmm_flows.csv has no procurement-contract-value field, so CO — the instrument most directly tied to awarded contracts — stands in as the nearest available proxy for "Round 1 principal", not the spec's exact percentage-of-contract-value definition of C1.

Rounds 2 and 3 are both derived from one published table (Economic Brief No. 2 s.2025, p. 27, PSA actuals at constant 2018 prices), and four accounting identities were checked against it first: supply-side sum equals GRDP; industry subsectors equal industry GVA; consumption plus capital formation plus net exports equals GRDP; exports minus imports equals net exports. All four close within the ±0.1 rounding of one-decimal published figures. Both are labelled medium confidence, never high: C2 divides a supply-side value-added figure by a demand-side expenditure figure, and C5 is a ceiling on all final demand, not household spend alone.

C3 (deposit capture and re-lending) has no slider, by design. It is not a within-round leak rate — it determines whether a fourth round happens at all, which is what the stop-at-three-rounds truncation assumes away. Its personnel-services-per-province half cannot be built: the province column is empty on all 427 rows of barmm_flows.csv. What is sourced instead supports the truncation: 91 of 116 municipalities unbanked, 47 banking offices region-wide, and a region-wide loans-to-deposits ratio of 5.6%.

Why "no bank" is a circularity finding, not just an access statistic

Where the one Shari'ah-compliant bank's financing actually goes, as a share of portfolio, 2024.

The chain, each link measured. 91 of 116 local governments have no banking office, and the region has 47 offices for 116 of them — 0.41 each — so salary and transfer money largely cannot be captured as a deposit. Where deposits are mobilised, re-lending is thin: region-wide, BARMM banks held ₱150.208B in deposits against ₱8.431B in loans, a ratio of 5.6%. AAIIBP, the country's only Islamic bank, re-lends 25.6% of its deposits and puts 88.11% of that portfolio into household activities and retail trade against 1.07% in construction. These figures show limited access and a concentrated portfolio; they are contextual evidence for the three-round truncation, not proof of a causal consumption pathway.
Corrections, denominators, and every figure with its source ▾

The spec's own table was transcribed wrongly, and this panel uses the corrected reading. Spec §3.5 lists Maguindanao del Sur 38 · Lanao del Sur 23 · Basilan 11 · Maguindanao del Norte 10 · Tawi-Tawi 9. The source chart, re-read as native text, gives Lanao del Sur 38 · Maguindanao del Sur 23 · Basilan 11 · Tawi-Tawi 10 · Maguindanao del Norte 9 — two pairs were swapped. Both readings sum to 91, so no checksum would have caught it. It matters because it inverts which province is the worst case.

Counts, not rates. The BSP-compatible denominator is region-wide only (116), which is why 78.4% appears once, for BARMM as a whole, and never per province. The sources disagree even there: the 2nd Bangsamoro Development Plan refers to 119 local governments, and PSA's July 2025 PSGC workbook gives 108 including the Special Geographic Area. A third disagreeing source does not adjudicate between the other two, so the region-wide-only framing stands.

Confidence on the AAIIBP row is low, on representativeness. It is one institution with nine branches nationwide, not BARMM-only, operating at a loss and mid-turnaround toward full Islamic-bank status. Its audited ratios are real figures about that bank and are not presented as a BARMM banking-system statistic.

Why C1 is not computable, and everything that was checked ▾

The spec defines C1 as the share of contract value going to BARMM-registered suppliers. Public data gives merchant counts with no linkage to award values or addresses, so a value-weighted share — and the concentration measures V10 calls for — cannot be derived without fabricating a distribution. None are shown, and registered address is not economic substance in any case.

Checked and rejected: MFBM's Summary of Awarded Contracts page (2022 and 2023 tabs, no files posted); PhilGEPS's public award-notice search (capped to a rolling 90-day window, no client-agency filter); PhilGEPS Open Data Power BI dashboards (pre-aggregated only, and several visuals do not respect the Region filter); MFBM's Procurement Monitoring Report as of 31 Dec 2025 (project costs and dates, no supplier column); the modernized-PhilGEPS bidding specification (a future capability, not a dataset); and a GPPB-TSO freedom-of-information response confirming the data is not held there.

07

What we cannot show yet

Named, defined, and openly unbuilt

Six things this dashboard is asked to show and does not. Each is left visibly absent rather than filled with a modelled stand-in, because in every case the invented number would sit on exactly the axis the chart exists to prove. Rechecked against fresh public sources on 11 August 2026.

Not found
C1 — procurement localisation by value
Public data gives merchant counts, never award value linked to a supplier address. So no value-weighted local share, no concentration curve, and no "leaked outside BARMM" node in the flow map. Confirmed not found again on recheck.
Not found
Personnel services by province
The province column is empty on all 427 rows: the source reports by instrument and by ministry, never by province. That blocks the salary-versus-bank-density overlay. A civil-service headcount was evaluated as a proxy and rejected — headcount not pesos, local-government staff only, and a pre-2024 province map.
Narrowed, not closed
Sub-annual timing of the Special Fund loop
Sixteen Special Fund SARO issue dates were found for FY2024 — the first release-side timestamps in this project. They are still not obligation or disbursement dates, and they cover one of three completed cohorts, so the lag stays at whole-fiscal-year resolution.
Partly found
C3 — deposit capture and re-lending
The banking half arrived: BARMM deposits ₱150.208B against loans ₱8.431B, March 2026. Region-wide only, so it corroborates the low re-lending rate without unblocking the province resolution C3 needs. No slider stands in for it.
Unresolved
The unbanked-rate denominator
Three sources give three counts of BARMM local governments — 116, 119 and 108. A third disagreeing figure does not adjudicate between the other two, so 78.4% is stated once, region-wide, and never per province.
Retired
The composite circularity index
Dead as specified: it requires a province dimension that exists nowhere in the data, and C1 is unrecoverable. Recorded as a decision in the methodology note rather than left implied.
Dataset-level gaps carried forward
  • Local government own-source revenue is invisible — only shares downloaded to them are tracked.
  • Private investment is not tracked at BARMM level at all.
  • Ministry reporting is asynchronous within a single year-end report; FY2024–25 columns are ceilings, not finals.
  • The Sulu discontinuity means post-2024 five-province figures are not comparable to pre-2024 six-province ones.
  • Provincial population, and therefore every per-capita fiscal metric, is not yet in the dataset.
